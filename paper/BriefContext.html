Leveraging Long Context in Retrieval Augmented Language Models for Medical Question Answering

Gongbo Zhang, Zihan Xu, Qiao Jin, Fangyi Chen, Yilu Fang, Yi Liu, Justin F. Rousseau, Ziyang Xu, Zhiyong Lu, Chunhua Weng, Yifan Peng

01Motivation

Retrieval-augmented models skim the middle of a long context

  • Retrieval grounds a medical answer in real literature — but only if the model reads the right passage.
  • Models attend to the start and end of a long context and skim the middle: the “lost-in-the-middle” problem.
  • The answer can sit in the retrieved set and still be missed, so accuracy depends on where the evidence lands.
02Method

BriefContext applies a map-reduce strategy over the retrieved context

  • BriefContext splits retrieved documents into smaller contexts, answers within each, then reduces the partial answers.
  • A preflight check predicts, before generation, whether a query is at risk.
  • No weight changes — it wraps any LLM backbone.
03Result

BriefContext maintains accuracy as retrieval depth increases

  • Vanilla RAG degrades as more documents are packed into the prompt.
  • BriefContext stays flat or improves across four different backbones as top-k rises.
  • Gains are largest exactly where vanilla RAG fails — key evidence buried mid-context.
04Result

BriefContext wins more head-to-head comparisons than it loses

  • Head-to-head against vanilla RAG, BriefContext wins far more comparisons than it loses.
  • The advantage holds at every position the key document occupies in the context.
05Result

The advantage widens as retrieval depth increases from top-8 to top-16

  • Going from top-k 8 to top-k 16 is exactly where vanilla RAG degrades most.
  • That is where the map-reduce split earns its keep.
06Result

A preflight check predicts lost-in-the-middle failures before generation

  • The check flags queries likely to suffer lost-in-the-middle before any answer is generated.
  • It is deliberately cautious — it would rather re-plan than let a failure through.
