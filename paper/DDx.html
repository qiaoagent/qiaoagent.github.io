01Motivation

Lab results guide real diagnosis but are absent from most LLM evaluations

  • Differential diagnosis is how clinicians separate conditions that present alike.
  • Most LLM diagnosis studies feed a narrative vignette — symptoms and history, but no lab values.
  • Real workups turn on the labs, so the open question is whether they change what the model concludes.
02Method

The study pairs fifty case reports with five LLMs, run with and without labs

  • Vignettes were built from 50 randomly selected PMC-Patients case reports.
  • Each was run with and without lab data through five LLMs.
  • Outputs were scored for exact and lenient accuracy against the true diagnosis.
03Cohort

The fifty cases span twelve medical categories

  • The cases span twelve medical categories, weighted toward gastrointestinal, infectious and endocrine disease.
  • That spread keeps the benchmark from measuring one specialty in disguise.

05Result

Lab data raises diagnostic accuracy by up to 30% across models

  • Across all five models, adding lab values raises both exact and lenient accuracy.
  • The improvement is statistically significant (Holm-adjusted p < 0.05), with GPT-4 and Mixtral gaining most.
06Result

The lab-informed advantage persists from Top-1 through Top-10

  • Accuracy trends upward for every model as more differentials are allowed.
  • The lab-informed curves sit above the lab-free ones throughout, not just at Top-1.
07Result

The improvement from lab data is statistically significant at every depth

  • Paired t tests compare each model with and without lab values, across the fifty cases.
  • Holm correction for multiple comparisons leaves every depth significant at p < 0.05.
