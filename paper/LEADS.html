01Motivation

Systematic reviews synthesise evidence but demand months of expert labour

  • A systematic review turns thousands of studies into one answer — and can take a team the better part of a year.
  • General LLMs offer help, but progress has been held back by the absence of training data built for the task.
  • The work is not one job but three: searching, screening, and extracting data from studies.
02Method

LEADS is trained on 633,759 samples curated from real systematic reviews

  • LEADSInstruct is assembled from 21,335 systematic reviews, 453,625 involved citations and 27,015 trial registries.
  • Those sources become instructions for the three tasks a reviewer actually performs.
03Result

LEADS retrieves more target studies than GPT-4o in publication and trial search

  • On both publication and trial search, LEADS recovers more of the target studies than GPT-4o.
  • The margin is widest on trial search, where general models struggle most.
04Result

LEADS achieves higher screening recall than dense retrieval and Mistral-7B

  • Against dense retrieval and Mistral-7B, LEADS recovers more of the target studies at every difficulty level.
  • The margin holds in the hardest bucket, where more than 30 studies must be found.
On the head-to-head recall table, GPT-4o edges LEADS at Recall@20 and Recall@100; LEADS leads at Recall@10 and Recall@50.
05Result

LEADS outperforms four frontier LLMs across all data extraction tasks

  • Across all four extraction tasks, LEADS leads GPT-4o, GPT-3.5, Haiku and Mistral under manual evaluation.
  • The largest margin is on participant statistics, the task general models handle worst.
06Result

Experts using LEADS achieve higher recall and accuracy in less time

  • Measured where it counts — a user study with 16 clinicians and researchers across 14 institutions.
  • The gain shows up as human–AI collaboration: experts stay in the loop and still move faster.

LEADS at a glance

BackgroundSystematic reviews are the backbone of evidence-based medicine.
ProblemThey are slow, and generic LLMs lack data built for the task.
ApproachA foundation model trained on 633,759 samples from real reviews, trials and registries.
